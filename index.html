Xuanbai Ren

I am a Ph.D. candidate at the school of computer science, Hunan University, under the supervision of Prof.Xiangxiang Zeng and Prof.Yiping Liu. Previously, I obtained my M.E. degree at the school of computer science, Hunan University in 2022, working with Prof.Lijun Cai and Prof.Xiangzheng Fu. I earned my B.E. degree from Hengyang Normal University in 2019.

Research Interest: My research focuses on AI-driven molecular and peptide discovery, with an emphasis on language models and multi-objective optimization.

  • Machine Learning: 1) Language Models, studying how model parameters shape generated molecular properties; 2) Multi-objective Optimization, balancing competing objectives in molecular and peptide design.

Publication & Activities: My research has been published in interdisciplinary journals and computer science conferences, including Nature Communications, Advanced Science, Bioinformatics, ACL, AAAI, NeurIPS, and CVPR. A full publication list can be found on Google Scholar.

Our deepAMP study identified the antimicrobial peptide T2-9, whose in vitro activity against E. coli matched that of the clinical antibiotic levofloxacin (MIC: 2.93 μg/mL for both). The designed peptides are covered by a granted Chinese invention patent arising from collaborative research.

Our HydrogelFinder study identified 9 hydrogel-forming peptides among 17 synthesized candidates (52.9%), completing the design-to-experiment workflow in 19 days. A HydrogelFinder-designed gel, PD-DP-008 (JIJ02), has entered Phase I clinical evaluation (CTR20250456).

I also serve as a reviewer for:

  • Conferences: ICLR (2024-2027), NeurIPS (2025-2026), AAAI (2026-2027)
  • Journals: Bioinformatics, Briefings in Bioinformatics, Scientific Reports.

Aug 30, 2024 🎉 Our paper “DeepAMP A Foundation Model Identifies Broad-Spectrum Antimicrobial Peptides against Drug-Resistant Bacterial Infection” is published in Nature Communications! In this study, we developed deepAMP, a peptide language-based deep generative framework designed to expedite the discovery of potent, broad-spectrum antimicrobial peptides (AMPs). By employing a novel sequence degradation strategy and multi-stage fine-tuning, we identified 29 high-potential candidates. These peptides proved effective in treating P. aeruginosa skin wound infections in mouse models with excellent biocompatibility. Notably, the identified peptide T2-9 showed in vitro activity against E. coli matching that of the clinical antibiotic levofloxacin, with the same MIC of 2.93 μg/mL.
May 5, 2024 🎉 Our work HydrogelFinder (A Foundation Model for Efficient Self-Assembling Peptide Discovery Guided by Non-Peptidal Small Molecules) is accepted to Advanced Science. By leveraging non-peptidal small molecules to guide chemical space exploration, we’ve achieved a rapid 19-day workflow from generation to wet-lab validation. 🧪 Our model successfully identified 9 novel self-assembling peptides (1-10 amino acids), including the shortest lipid-peptide documented to date. PD-DP-008 (JIJ02), has entered Phase I clinical evaluation (CTR20250456).
